How ranking works

Every item is kept. Ranking only decides what comes first.

The four steps

  1. The record comes first. If the IMM sponsored a revision request, filed comments, stated a position in ERCOT's report, or filed in a PUC control number, the item is high.
  2. Everything else gets a score from 0 to 100. The score says how closely the item's wording matches items the IMM engaged on, weighed against items it passed over. It is learned from the record below, not from a hand-written keyword list. Bills and notices leave no IMM record of their own, so a bill is scored the way a PUC project on the same subject would be and an ERCOT notice the way a protocol revision would be; their score is then their percentile among electric bills, or among notices.
  3. The score sets the rank. The cut-offs are placed so that, on items the model had never seen, 9 in 10 of those the IMM engaged on come out medium or high and 6 in 10 come out high.
  4. Settled items stay low. An item that closed on or before October 31, 2026 without an IMM comment is ranked low whatever its score. Anything still open is ranked by its score.
